# EEO Language Guardrails

Use these guardrails for homepage copy, dossier text, claim cards, evidence summaries, release notes, press materials, and internal-to-public translations.

EEO is evidence for inquiry, not a verdict.

## Preferred posture

Use language that says EEO:

- documents available evidence;
- makes visible;
- connects claims to sources;
- identifies uncertainty;
- explains source limits;
- supports public-interest inquiry;
- allows correction;
- enables right of reply;
- uses public-safe summaries;
- aggregates, generalizes, restricts, or withholds sensitive information where exposure could cause harm.

## Avoid language that implies EEO

Do not say or imply that EEO:

- proves illegality;
- assigns guilt;
- adjudicates ownership, title, sovereignty, responsibility, liability, or harm;
- certifies stewardship;
- ranks countries, companies, communities, governments, or projects;
- verifies physical origin from trade data alone;
- proves causation from spatial proximity alone;
- creates legal, moral, or public legitimacy through technology;
- owns, reallocates, validates, or governs resources;
- replaces courts, Indigenous governance, public agencies, statistical systems, EITI, SEEA, ResourceContracts, Open Ownership, Global Forest Watch, Trase, UN Comtrade, ILOSTAT, USGS, national statistical systems, or other competent authorities.

## Safe public sentence pattern

Use this structure for claim summaries:

```text
Available evidence documents [finding]. It supports [limited interpretation]. It does not prove [non-proof]. EEO publishes this as evidence for inquiry, not a verdict.
```

## Safer replacements

Prefer:

- `available public data indicate`
- `reported`
- `source-linked evidence suggests`
- `supports a limited interpretation`
- `does not establish`
- `does not by itself prove`
- `under review`
- `challenged`
- `restricted`
- `withdrawn`
- `source limitation`
- `public-safe summary`
- `right-of-reply pathway`
- `correction pathway`

Avoid unless supported by authoritative legal/regulatory findings and publication review:

- `guilty`
- `liable`
- `illegal`
- `corrupt`
- `corruption proven`
- `violation established`
- `caused harm`
- `owns this resource`
- `proves`
- `traceable to this mine`
- `official finding`
- `convicted`
- `responsible for harm`

## Claim-card checklist

Before publishing or changing a public claim, verify that the claim:

- names or links the source;
- explains the source limit;
- states confidence or review status;
- distinguishes direct evidence from contextual evidence;
- states what the evidence does not prove;
- has a correction pathway;
- has right-of-reply review where it materially affects an identifiable actor;
- has map-safety or disclosure review where spatial or sensitive information is involved;
- avoids public exposure of vulnerable people, communities, sacred sites, sensitive habitats, whistleblowers, restricted community data, or exact sensitive coordinates.

## Public/private translation rule

Internal notes must not be copied directly to public surfaces. Translate them into public-safe language that describes:

- what is known;
- what is uncertain;
- what is withheld or generalized;
- why a limitation exists;
- how correction or reply can occur.
