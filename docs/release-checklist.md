# EEO Release Checklist

Use this checklist before publishing, amplifying, or materially changing public EEO surfaces.

The release gate does not adjudicate truth, wrongdoing, liability, title, sovereignty, ownership, or legitimacy. It asks whether publication conditions have been satisfied.

## 1. Scope check

Confirm the change stays inside the current corridor-first product boundary.

The change must not introduce:

- global atlas expansion;
- public composite scores, rankings, or certification marks;
- public monitoring dashboards, live feeds, alerts, or forecasts;
- scenario or temporal profile UI;
- blockchain trust claims;
- AI judgment or legal-decision surfaces;
- public community reporting modules;
- new public exposure of sensitive communities, locations, identities, or restricted evidence.

## 2. Source and evidence check

For each public claim, confirm:

- source is identified;
- license or use basis is known or marked unknown;
- source date is present where applicable;
- evidence role is clear;
- source limitations are visible;
- confidence or review status is visible;
- stale-after or review timing is recorded where applicable;
- claim does not use trade data as chain-of-custody proof;
- claim does not use spatial proximity as causation proof.

## 3. Disclosure and map-safety check

Confirm the release does not expose:

- exact vulnerable locations;
- sacred sites;
- sensitive Indigenous or community knowledge without authority, consent, context, and benefit rules;
- endangered species or fragile habitat coordinates;
- artisanal mining settlements or worker-sensitive details;
- whistleblower, submitter, or household-level information;
- critical infrastructure or security-sensitive details;
- internal notes, legal review notes, or private evidence packets.

Where risk exists, use public-safe summaries, aggregation, generalization, restriction, withholding, or non-collection.

## 4. Right-of-reply check

Consider right-of-reply review when a public claim identifies or materially affects:

- a company;
- a government agency;
- a concession holder;
- an operator;
- a public institution;
- an organization;
- another identifiable actor.

Right-of-reply is especially important when a claim implies:

- misconduct;
- missing disclosure;
- public harm;
- unresolved risk;
- benefit failure;
- ownership/control concern;
- labor risk;
- ecological harm;
- public revenue issue;
- concession-governance issue.

Right-of-reply is publication discipline, not a concession that a claim is false.

## 5. Language-safety check

Confirm public copy:

- says or implies evidence for inquiry, not a verdict;
- avoids guilt, liability, illegality, corruption, proof, causation, traceability, ownership, ranking, certification, and adjudication language unless supported by authoritative findings and approved for publication;
- states what the evidence does not prove;
- links or points to correction and reply routes where relevant;
- distinguishes source limitation from finding.

## 6. Release-manifest check

Before a public release or public-facing amplification, confirm the release manifest or equivalent public-safe release record states:

- release scope;
- evidence objects included;
- claim objects included;
- source limitation status;
- disclosure or map-safety status;
- right-of-reply status where applicable;
- correction status where applicable;
- public-safe limitations;
