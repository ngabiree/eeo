# Earth Endowment Observatory
## Canonical Institutional, Product, and Web-App Systems Specification v2.0
### One comprehensive build document

**Motto:** From Earth to economy, made visible.

**Canonical doctrine:** Universal in analytical concern. Plural in governance. Tiered in disclosure. Strict in evidence. Humble in technology. Relentless in public explanation.

**Build posture:** Controlled evidence product first. Limited dashboard second. Broader knowledge commons only after trust is earned.

**Current available stack:** Supabase, Vercel, GitHub, and AI assistants.

---

# 0. Executive repair verdict

The Earth Endowment Observatory is strong enough to build, but it must now stop expanding outward and consolidate inward.

The repaired version is this:

**EEO is a public-interest institution and web system that connects existing evidence about natural endowments, governance, labor, trade, ecological condition, ownership, public revenue, and value capture. It does not claim authority over territory, resources, peoples, states, firms, or courts. It helps societies see how endowments become value, who bears risk, who captures benefit, what evidence supports each claim, what remains uncertain, and what should not be exposed.**

The first web product should not be a full global atlas, public complaint system, AI interface, ESG dashboard, global score, blockchain system, or universal resource registry.

The first web product should be:

1. **one flagship evidence dossier**;
2. **one limited corridor dashboard**;
3. **one evidence ledger**;
4. **one internal review workspace**;
5. **one right-of-reply and correction workflow**;
6. **one signed release manifest**;
7. **one safe corridor pilot**.

The first build loop is:

```text
source -> license -> evidence -> claim -> entity resolution -> review -> exposure review -> right-of-reply if needed -> release manifest -> public evidence dossier -> correction route
```

That loop is the true MVP.

# 2. Canonical identity

## 2.1 What EEO is

EEO is a rights-aware, evidence-disciplined, federated public-interest observatory.

It connects existing systems and sources across:

- natural endowments;
- geography;
- legal authority;
- concessions and permits;
- contracts and obligations;
- corporate and beneficial ownership;
- trade and processing pathways;
- labor conditions and worker risk;
- ecological indicators;
- public revenue;
- community benefit;
- restoration and intergenerational liability;
- evidence gaps and uncertainty.

Its authority is civic and epistemic: disciplined evidence, careful explanation, traceable claims, safe disclosure, and independent review.

## 2.2 What EEO is not

EEO is not:

- a world government;
- a global resource owner;
- a universal ownership registry;
- a redistribution engine;
- an adjudicatory tribunal;
- a resource certification system;
- an ESG scoring product;
- a blockchain trust layer;
- a public exposure engine;
- a replacement for SEEA, EITI, ResourceContracts, Open Ownership, Global Forest Watch, Trase, UN Comtrade, ILOSTAT, USGS, national statistical systems, indigenous institutions, courts, or public agencies.

## 2.3 The repaired public sentence

**The Earth Endowment Observatory makes visible how natural endowments become economic value, who governs and transforms them, who captures benefit, who bears risk, what evidence supports each claim, and where stewardship, rights, and public accountability are strong, weak, uncertain, or hidden.**

## 2.4 The line EEO must not cross

EEO must never convert moral concern into unauthorized institutional control.

It may trace evidence-supported relationships in an endowment-to-economy system. It may not present that trace as physical chain of custody or claim authority over the people, places, institutions, materials, or legal rights involved.

# 3. Constitutional doctrine in operational form

## 3.1 Universal in analytical concern

EEO may study natural-endowment systems and value chains wherever they create public, ecological, labor, fiscal, or intergenerational consequences.

This does not create authority over peoples, states, territories, communities, firms, courts, or resources.

## 3.2 Plural in governance

EEO must show overlapping governance orders without collapsing them:

- earth-system limits;
- indigenous rights and authority;
- national sovereignty;
- local stewardship;
- labor rights;
- private enterprise and contract;
- public finance;
- intergenerational duty.

## 3.3 Tiered in disclosure

Universal knowledge does not require universal exposure.

Some evidence should be public. Some should be aggregated. Some should be restricted. Some should be community-governed. Some should be suppressed. Some should not be collected at all.

## 3.4 Strict in evidence

Every public claim must have:

- source;
- source license or use basis;
- date;
- method;
- confidence label;
- evidence layer;
- granularity class;
- legal posture;
- disclosure tier;
- stale-after date;
- review status;
- correction path.

## 3.5 Humble in technology

Technology does not create legitimacy. EEO should prefer conventional, inspectable, secure systems over fashionable complexity.

Supabase, Vercel, GitHub, and AI assistants are sufficient for the first institutional build if used with discipline.

## 3.6 Relentless in public explanation

Data that cannot be understood cannot support accountability.

Every public module must explain:

- what is known;
- what is uncertain;
- what is disputed;
- what is restricted;
- how the claim was made;
- how it can and cannot be used.

# 4. Red lines

EEO shall not:

1. claim that all resources legally belong to humanity as an undifferentiated body;
2. adjudicate title, liability, legality, consent, or fault;
3. publish sacred-site, whistleblower, vulnerable-community, endangered-species, or sensitive ecological-location data without authority and protection;
4. publish exact sensitive geospatial coordinates where aggregation, masking, or non-public treatment is safer;
5. publish named high-impact allegations without a right-of-reply workflow;
6. call trade statistics “traceability” unless chain-of-custody evidence supports that term;
7. treat public revenue as proof of public benefit;
8. treat formal legal authority as proof of full legitimacy;
9. convert proxy labor data into site-level labor certainty;
10. launch a composite score, country ranking, company seal, or stewardship index in the pilot;
11. allow pay-to-improve treatment;
12. allow donor, corporate, state, or ideological control over methods or findings;
13. use AI to make final publication, legal, confidence, or disclosure decisions;
14. build a public community-reporting module before protection protocols are mature;
15. use blockchain as institutional identity or trust substitute.

# 8. Evidence standard

## 8.1 Four publication layers

EEO must keep four layers separate.

### Factual layer
What is directly documented by a source.

Allowed phrasing:

- “Public records state...”
- “The cited source reports...”
- “The official disclosure lists...”

### Analytical layer
What EEO derives through comparison, modeling, spatial overlay, entity resolution, or synthesis.

Allowed phrasing:

- “Available data suggest...”
- “This estimate is derived from...”
- “This relationship is inferred from...”

### Normative layer
Why a pattern raises stewardship, labor, public-benefit, rights, or accountability questions.

Allowed phrasing:

- “This raises a public-benefit question because...”
- “This indicates an accountability gap...”
- “This may matter for procurement, due diligence, or public oversight...”

### Legal layer
What a competent legal authority has found, alleged, or not determined.

Allowed phrasing:

- “EEO makes no legal finding.”
- “The cited authority found...”
- “The cited complaint alleges...”
- “The legal status is disputed.”

## 8.1.1 Canonical authority and tracing distinctions

EEO must keep five concepts separate in every public record, method note, and institutional statement:

1. **Evidence-supported tracing** means a visible, reviewable path from a public statement to its evidence records, sources, methods, limitations, and correction history. It traces the basis of a claim; it does not establish the movement, custody, origin, or identity of physical material.
2. **Physical chain of custody** means documented control or transfer of material through relevant custody events. Production statistics, customs data, corporate disclosures, spatial proximity, or an EEO evidence path do not establish physical chain of custody on their own. EEO may cite a chain-of-custody record but does not certify it.
3. **Legal findings** are determinations, orders, judgments, or formal findings made by a competent court, regulator, tribunal, or other legally authorized body. EEO makes no legal finding, assigns no liability, and reports the issuing authority, status, scope, and limits of any cited finding.
4. **Ethical principles** guide EEO's own stewardship, rights, harm, public-benefit, consent, and intergenerational analysis. They support questions and institutional choices; they are not legal verdicts, compliance determinations, or certification criteria.
5. **Institutional authority** is limited to EEO's own evidence, review, disclosure, correction, and release practices. EEO's authority is civic and epistemic, not sovereign, judicial, regulatory, territorial, or certifying.

Public language must use **evidence-supported tracing** where the claim-to-source path is meant. It must not shorten that concept to **traceability** when a reasonable reader could understand physical origin, custody, certification, or regulatory assurance.

## 8.2 Claim taxonomy

Each claim must include three different classifications.

### Claim type
- Observed
- Official
- Modeled
- Inferred
- Alleged
- Disputed
- Confidential
- Withdrawn

### Confidence label
- Verified
- Official
- Modeled
- Estimated
- Self-reported
- Third-party reported
- Community-reported
- Disputed
- Restricted
- Outdated
- Unknown

### Evidence layer
- Factual
- Analytical
- Normative
- Legal

## 8.3 Granularity class

Every claim must indicate granularity:

- site-level;
- facility-level;
- corridor-level;
- district-level;
- province-level;
- national-level;
- regional-level;
- global-level;
- unknown.

## 8.4 Legal posture

Every claim with possible reputational or legal consequence must include legal posture:

- no legal claim;
- public-record description;
- allegation by source;
- finding by authority;
- disputed legal status;
- requires legal review.

## 8.5 Safer language guardrails

Do not say:

- “Company X violated...” unless an authoritative legal source has found that.
- “This route proves origin...” unless chain-of-custody evidence supports it.
- “This community consented...” unless authority and applicable protocol support it.
- “This value distribution is unjust...” as a platform verdict.
- “This mine caused...” where only proximity or correlation exists.

Prefer:

- “Available public records indicate...”
- “The evidence is insufficient to determine...”
- “This is a risk factor, not a legal finding.”
- “Reported trade data does not prove physical traceability.”
- “Disclosed public revenue does not prove durable public benefit.”
- “Spatial proximity does not establish causation without additional evidence.”

# 9. Data governance and disclosure law

## 9.1 Disclosure tiers

### Tier 0 — Open
Low foreseeable harm; lawful; clear public-interest value.

Handling: public display, API, citation, possible download.

### Tier 1 — Contextual public
Publicly usable but requires caveat, uncertainty, right-of-reply status, or method note.

Handling: public display with warnings and provenance.

### Tier 2 — Aggregated
Raw form creates location, identity, ecological, or retaliation risk.

Handling: aggregate by geography, time, actor, or category.

### Tier 3 — Verified access
Useful for research or review but too sensitive for public release.

Handling: controlled access, purpose limits, logging, expiration.

### Tier 4 — Community-governed
Subject to indigenous, local, or rights-holder authority.

Handling: governed by consent protocol and authority-specific rules.

### Tier 5 — Suppressed
Publication likely enables harm or violates rights.

Handling: do not publish. Store only if necessary and protected, or do not retain.

## 9.2 Movement between tiers

Data may move to a less restrictive tier only after explicit review and approval.

Movement from Tier 3, 4, or 5 to public release requires:

- documented public-interest justification;
- renewed harm review;
- legal review where relevant;
- rights-holder authority where required;
- audit log entry;
- release note where safe.

## 9.3 What disclosure is based on

Disclosure is not based only on legality.

It is based on:

- legality;
- rights;
- consent;
- safety;
- sensitivity;
- public interest;
- possible misuse;
- reversibility;
- granularity;
- confidence;
- correction ability.

# 11. Right-of-reply and named actor protocol

## 11.1 Required before named high-impact publication

Right-of-reply is required before public release where EEO names a specific actor in relation to high-impact risk, including:

- named company risk profile;
- named public agency risk profile;
- named beneficial owner risk profile;
- named operator linked to unresolved allegation;
- named actor connected to severe labor, ecological, corruption, or public-benefit risk;
- contested ownership/control claim with reputational consequence.

## 11.2 Workflow

```text
draft claim -> identify named actor risk -> prepare evidence packet -> send notice -> wait for response window -> summarize response -> revise/withdraw/defer/publish -> display right-of-reply status
```

## 11.3 Public statuses

Public pages may show:

- response received and summarized;
- no response received by deadline;
- claim revised after response;
- claim withdrawn after response;
- publication deferred pending review.

## 11.4 No named allegation rule

No named allegation layer may be published until this workflow is functioning.

# 12. Map-safety protocol

## 12.1 Map publication principle

A map is not neutral. A map can protect, but it can also expose.

All geospatial publication must pass map-safety review.

## 12.2 Publication modes

Each map layer must be assigned one mode:

- exact geometry public;
- generalized geometry public;
- aggregated to administrative unit;
- blurred or masked;
- delayed release;
- restricted access only;
- metadata-only public;
- do not collect;
- do not publish.

## 12.3 Sensitive map defaults

Presume sensitivity for:

- sacred sites;
- indigenous/community-held knowledge;
- endangered species locations;
- vulnerable habitats;
- artisanal mining settlements;
- whistleblower or grievance locations;
- community reports;
- critical infrastructure;
- illegal extraction targets;
- locations where publication could trigger retaliation, speculation, poaching, or land grabbing.

## 12.4 Public map UX

Every public map layer must show:

- source;
- date;
- resolution;
- confidence;
- whether geometry was generalized or masked;
- why detail may be suppressed.

## 12.5 Download rule

No geospatial downloads in the MVP unless the layer is explicitly approved as Tier 0 or Tier 1 and the license permits redistribution.

- public product;
- restricted workspace;
- source registry;
- evidence vault;

# 17. Supabase architecture

## 17.1 Supabase responsibility

Supabase owns the institutional data plane:

- users and roles;
- canonical tables;
- RLS policies;
- source registry;
- source licenses;
- evidence metadata;
- private evidence files;
- claims;
- entity aliases and identifiers;
- review tasks;
- exposure reviews;
- right-of-reply workflow;
- release manifests;
- public released views;
- audit events;
- PostGIS spatial metadata.

## 17.2 Supabase should not own

- final editorial judgment;
- legal conclusions;
- final disclosure decisions;
- final safeguards decisions;
- public narrative authority without human review.

## 17.3 Schemas

Use separate database schemas:

```text
core       -- canonical records: sources, licenses, entities, claims, map layers
review     -- review tasks, exposure reviews, right-of-reply, releases, audit events
private    -- restricted notes and sensitive objects, if needed
analytics  -- indicator cards and derived snapshots
public     -- released public views only
```

## 17.4 Storage buckets

Use these buckets:

### `public-release-assets`
Approved public images, diagrams, method files, static release assets.

### `evidence-vault`
Raw evidence, source snapshots, documents, and review materials. Private by default.

### `restricted-review`
Sensitive or pre-publication materials. Private, logged, and access-limited.

### `right-of-reply-packets`
Evidence packets prepared for actor response. Private and time-limited.

## 17.5 RLS doctrine

RLS must enforce the true boundary. UI route guards are not sufficient.

Rules:

- anonymous users read only public released views;
- authenticated public contributors may submit corrections but not view restricted data;
- researchers may read assigned workspace data;
- reviewers may access objects assigned to their role;
- release owners may assemble releases but cannot bypass blocking reviews;
- admins can manage configuration but all sensitive actions are audited;

# 20. Core workflows

## 20.1 Source intake

```text
researcher creates source -> license/use basis recorded -> sensitivity noted -> integration status set -> audit event created
```

No source proceeds to release-bound use without license/use-basis review.

## 20.2 Evidence upload

```text
source selected -> evidence object uploaded to private bucket -> hash generated -> metadata attached -> disclosure tier assigned -> audit event created
```

## 20.3 Claim drafting

```text
evidence inspected -> claim drafted -> source/method attached -> claim type assigned -> evidence layer assigned -> confidence assigned -> granularity assigned -> legal posture assigned -> disclosure tier assigned -> stale-after date assigned
```

## 20.4 Entity resolution

```text
entity candidate created -> alias/identifier checked -> possible match generated -> manual review if uncertain -> decision logged -> public display follows approved identity status
```

## 20.5 Review

```text
draft claim -> method review -> legal review if needed -> safeguards review if needed -> exposure review if needed -> labor/ecology review if needed -> editorial review -> approved/deferred/rejected
```

## 20.6 Map publication

```text
map layer registered -> sensitivity class assigned -> exposure review -> publication mode decided -> masking/aggregation applied -> metadata public -> geometry public only if approved
```

## 20.7 Right-of-reply

```text
named high-impact claim flagged -> evidence packet prepared -> notice sent -> response window -> response logged -> claim revised/withdrawn/deferred/published -> public status displayed
```

## 20.8 Release

```text
approved claims selected -> license check -> review check -> exposure check -> right-of-reply check -> release manifest generated -> release owner signs -> public views updated -> correction route opens
```

## 20.9 Correction

```text
public challenge submitted -> triage -> safety check -> assigned reviewer -> claim revised/confirmed/withdrawn -> public correction note if accepted -> audit trail retained
```

## 34.5 Licensing recommendation

Use separate licenses for code and content.

### Code license
Recommended default:

```text
Apache License 2.0
```

Why:

- permissive;
- institution-friendly;
- compatible with commercial, nonprofit, academic, and public-sector reuse;
- includes explicit patent language.

Alternative:

```text
MIT License
```

MIT is simpler, but Apache 2.0 is stronger for a public-interest platform that may attract institutional partners.

### Documentation/content license
Recommended default:

```text
Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0)
```

Use for public documentation, methods text, non-sensitive diagrams, and public explainers.

### Data license
Do not apply one blanket open-data license to all EEO data.

Use release-specific data licenses because source licensing, partner restrictions, and disclosure tiers vary.

Recommended data posture:

- public metadata: permissive with attribution where allowed;
- public claim ledger: CC BY 4.0 or similar only if source licenses allow;
- raw source-derived datasets: release-specific license;
- restricted data: no public license;
- community-governed data: governed by specific agreement;
- sensitive data: not published.

Add this statement to `DATA_POLICY.md`:

```text
Code in this repository may be open source, but EEO data is not open by default. Public data releases are governed by source licenses, disclosure tiers, rights review, safety review, and release-specific terms.
```

# 35. Final architecture law

EEO should be built as a governance-first claims platform.

Not a data dump.

Not a dashboard factory.

Not a global score.

Not a universal ownership map.

Not a blockchain project.

Not an AI authority.

The first platform must prove one disciplined civic loop:

**Can EEO take a source, understand its license, preserve the evidence, convert it into a claim, review it, protect against exposure harm, offer right-of-reply where needed, publish it with humility, and correct it when challenged?**

If yes, the institution can grow.

If no, the institution should not scale.

**The chain must be made visible without making vulnerable people, places, species, or knowledge more vulnerable.**
